# Roguefort 0.34.0 — Patch Notes

Hello Affineurs,

I'm thrilled to be bringing you Roguefort 0.34.0, jam packed with a few things I couldn't get done before Early Access launched, as well as a whole bunch of bug fixes and QoL changes, along with some community suggestions fulfilled. 

Thanks to everyone who has submitted a bug report or suggestion. I'm sorry I can't reply to each one but I am grateful for every single one and if I haven't gotten back to you, I'll drop a note as soon as I can, or just check the patch notes below for your name :-)

The next week I will continue with bug fixing, but I am going to start research in earnest how to get MOD SUPPORT going for the game. This will provide something for the modders who are already doing mind-bending unofficial mods, and provide the level of moddability and creative freedom to modify the game that I promised in the roadmap as we chug along to 1.0 in October next year.

Thanks for playing and have a safe and happy weekend.

Tim

## A new map of the world

- The world map is now a pixel art fantasy map rather than the placeholder vector-y looking map I had in there before. While easier to draw and render, it was not what I wanted. BUT NOW the map has proper pixelated mountain ranges, forests, coastlines and towns. It's the new default for every save and doesn't affect anything other than how the map looks and feels. 
- Zooming glides with the mouse wheel or trackpad and settles on whole pixels, from the whole continent right down to the tiles around you. The map has four levels of detail and fades from one to the next as you go in, hopefully smooth as butter
- Quest marks on the world map are solid pins with a gold rim now where they used to be to small faint symbols that weren't that readable. (todesbart)
- The new map style works across other games modes too so it's no just Adventure mode 
- Some technical stuff to make map rendering smoother and snappier in the old feel department
- Oh and also you can always switch back to the old map, if you really want to, as it's in the settings. 

## The minimap

- The minimap shows an arrow for the way you're facing, which should make first person a lot easier to find your way around in. (Bamboodium, Val, JLOST, juh9870)
- Places, waystones, your home and quest pins on the minimap use the same pixel icons as the world map. It's all about consistency, if I can remember to do it 
- The arrow at the minimap's edge that points to something off the screen used to sit on top of its own distance at large text size, but now the distance sits beside it now.

## Big screens and small screens

- Playing on a 4K screen at 100% Windows scaling the maps used to shrink into a corner while the world grew. The new map size setting (Settings -> Display -> Maps) fixes that, such that  "auto" doubles the minimap, the world map and everything on them at 4K, or you can pick anything from 100% to 300%. At 1080p, on laptops and on the Steam Deck nothing changes. (todesbart)
- The new Interface size setting (Settings -> Display -> Interface) grows the whole interface, including the top bar, the quick bar, the log and every menu, while the world view keeps its size. Auto picks 200% on a 4K screen at 100% scaling and 100% at 1080p and on the Deck. (todesbart)
- Interface size goes down to 75% and 90% as well, for a small screen at a high Windows scaling where the skill slots took up half the screen. (Lodin)

## Learning to make cheese

- One of the most dense and confusing systems of the game got a glow up! Cheesemaking is taught in a much more accessible and easy to understand way now. The Cheesewright's lesson tutorial provides players six steps from building a Cheese Vat to eating your first Herb Curd (GMJoe, xxturiflower47xx)
- If you already did the totes then a travelling affineur will walk up to you once and offer the course and you can do it or decline it 
- Every village in a new world has a herder, so the goat milk the course sends you for is always somewhere to be found, where before it wasn't so reliable to find 

## The Cheese Vat, the Cheese Press and the bench

- The Cheese Vat gets a glow up too. Now it lets the player know the milk and the culture it's about to use, says both get used up and how often the culture comes back, and has a Start button. (danekjovaxutc5, rovingrover, obiwankentucky, tthfry)
- Hover or Inspect any recipe in the Cheese Press to see the card of the cheese it makes at your curd's quality before you press it. The press has a Press button too. (danekjovaxutc5, hyperx69, rovingrover)
- On the affineur's bench, the recipe sits beside a fixed "What this wheel does" card with the cost underneath. Every field explains itself by hover, keys or pad, and the bench has its own first-time card and Help page. (jojoknob, tetraphyte, tthfry, ungyhy)
- Your wheels, a new page on the bench, lists the wheels you've pressed, and they sit at the top of the press's known cheeses list as well.
- When the bench hides a heart herb it tells you which curd that herb needs. (madness5520, ghraemdal, nalathnidragon)
- Fine and better cheeses show on their cards how long the effect really lasts at that quality. (hyperx69)
- One bump of a milking stool milks every ready animal of yours in a 5x5 square, unless a wall is in the way, and also it tells you once what you got. (madness5520, ghraemdal)

## Cheese recipes and the Recipe Book

- Herbalists teach the meadow cheeses earlier. A village herbalist's Herb Curd or Blossom Curd commission also teaches the Wrinkled, Vanilla and Shimmerleaf Chevres
- In the Recipe Book, the Cheesemaking page shows the curd styles each press takes, ticks off the flora you're carrying and gives the press and ageing times. Pick any cheese you know to read how it's made from start to finish. (danekjovaxutc5, hyperx69, Hlord)
- Help's Cheesemaking page is a bit more of a proper reference manual now and has new page for the affineur's bench and a cheese section in the glossary.
- Every cheese item in your pack says what it's for. A vat curd names the press and the quality it makes, a Herb Curd says no press takes it, and a culture also states which curd it makes.
- Vat curds are filed under Materials in the pack, chests and shops, since they go into a press and aren't for eating.
- Castable cheese is called War Cheese everywhere and the build list calls the stations the Cheese Vat, Cheese Press and Cheese Cave like everything else does

## Ageing and affineur rank

- Every finished cheese counts once toward affineur rank, including wheels off an ageing rack or a moon rack and cheese made at a workbench.
- Finishing a tier 4 wheel's ageing in your pack earns A Wheel Takes Its Voice, and a Master Affineur cheese aged in the pack counts toward the set. A war wheel pressed at a press can't stand in for a set cheese any more.
- "Your X has finished ageing!" shows once when a wheel finishes in your pack. In co-op it shows only on the screen of the player whose wheel it is.
- Two grades of the same cheese stay in two stacks, so a Masterwork wheel can't join a Fine stack and change its grade. The Old Mouth vault keeps them apart too. Stacks that already merged in a save stay merged. (tthfry)
- Cheese that finishes ageing in your pack joins the stack you already carry, and a padlocked wheel stays padlocked. (pcspiker)
- Arcane Rennet is always used up as its card has always said.
- "Learned a cheese recipe" only shows when you've actually learned one.
- The ageing rack tells you when its spot doesn't count for the long lay. Only a rack in tundra or taiga moves a Winter sleep wheel. (madness5520)

## The Hall of the Fallen

- Every Permadeath and Hardcore character you lose gets a grave in the Hall of the Fallen, a new page in Records on the title screen. Pick a grave to see its card, with the character drawn in their own gear. Graves are kept apart from your saves and shared through Steam Cloud.
- A one-life game over is a grave of its own now: GAME OVER, your character beside a gravestone, "Here lies" with their name and level, the run's figures, and Final moments, the last ten turns of the fight that killed them. (ravenor.)
- Game over screens name what killed you now. They used to show the last line of the log, which is how a character killed in their sleep ended up with "Too dangerous to rest" as the cause. (corestar)
- Return to Menu stays on screen on a Steam Deck however long the character's name is.

## Skills, pets and powers
  
- Vampires can store blood now. Fill an empty Glass Vial from your blood meter to make Bottled Blood, 25 in and 25 out, and it never takes you below Starving.
- Charm someone as a vampire and they ignore your trespassing for a while. Thick forest halves the sun's damage too.
- Stealth ranks all work now: Skulker's cover bonus, Shadow's sight while sneaking, Phantom slipping past sleepers and making a searching foe give up after 3 turns, and Nightblade getting past keen senses (but not a boss's).
- Eight companion abilities work now: the dog's bark, the hound's swimming, the canary's warning, the cat slipping through trees and rocks, the wolf's night damage, the stag's ring, the golem's cheese heal and the sporeling's map.
- Killing cave folk costs you standing, and cave staff see you steal.
- Every aura you wear ticks, and Terroir Attunement always adds at least 1.

## Sleeping, now quite a bit safer 

- Any hit wakes you from a rest now no matter where it comes from. A slinger shooting through a hut's door could kill a sleeper at full health without waking them. In co-op, a hurt partner wakes the whole party. All for one and one for all!!! (corestar, moleuk, ghraemdal)

## Smoother movement

- Holding a direction key in 2D could sort of jerk the view every few steps. Your character now stays put on screen while the world moves under them.
- Held movement used to rubber-band, worst when zoomed out and in a long save. Fixed. (4pt)
- With Hop or Slide movement, the first step after standing still skipped its animation on slower PCs, but now it glides now. (shtacks, madness5520)
- Walking past a townsperson who's asleep, hiding or on their way somewhere swaps places with a glide, where it used to jump a tile.
- Sleeping through the night won't pop up "performance issue detected" any more. (pcspiker)
- Carrying a sealing wheel cost a whole extra redraw of the game on every step, and that's gone. Snow and leaves are worked out once a day instead of every turn, which helps most when you're zoomed out. (4pt, bobinhedgeorge, malmortical, harmonixer3811, moleuk)
- In first person and 2.5D, walking through a town lit by torches could freeze the game for a moment at each new light, noooo. But fixed now. (bobinhedgeorge)
- First person and 2.5D skip rebuilding the scene when nothing in it has changed, which cuts the work while travelling, in co-op most of all. (bobinhedgeorge)
- Opening the world map shows a LOADING window until it's ready, and it opens faster. The first open after a load used to show half a map. (madness5520, petter7980, ghraemdal)

- Townsfolk show their asleep, at work and walking bubbles, and stockpile zones and a building's reach show on the ground.
