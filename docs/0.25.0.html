Hey everyone,

First, a huge thank you to the playtesters and community. A special thanks to NalathniDragon, whose mockups became the new inventory, and to SamV, Danek and knifee for all the discussion that shaped it. Thanks also to everyone who contributed bug reports and suggestions, all credited and detailed below. Thank you so much again!

This is a big one. The headlines:

  • A new inventory. Every row has its buttons in the same places, and every button works from the keyboard or a controller.
  • Fifteen village tutors who teach the game by having you play it, with a new Lessons panel to keep track.
  • Dungeons in new worlds have a history and moving parts, with locked doors and keys, levers and portcullises, hidden pressure plates and idol vaults.
  • Villages, towns and cities that feel lived in, with homes that show who lives there and townsfolk who talk among themselves.
  • Fortress goes 25 levels deep, with dread, forges, squads, flowing water and magma, a storyteller and war.
  • Redrawn roofs, lit windows at night and the sounds of forges, inns and markets, in every save.

Dungeons, town life and the deep Fortress need a new game or a new colony. Older saves keep every place they've already visited exactly as it was, and places you haven't reached yet are built the new way. The tutors, roofs, night lights and sounds come to every save.

New players and the village tutors11

  • Every new game has fifteen village tutors in the village nearest your start, one for each part of the game, from the Cheesewright to the Wayfinder.
  • Each lesson is a real quest you finish by doing the thing. The tutor hands you whatever you need to try it, each lesson pays a reward and 15 XP, and finishing all fifteen earns 40 gold, 40 XP and two Herb Curds.
  • Tutors with a lesson to give wear an open book over their heads, which turns into a gold ? when you can hand the lesson in. They show on the minimap and the world map, and Tab steps through them.
  • The new Lessons panel lists every tutor, where each lesson stands and what to do next.
  • Tutors speak in the voice of their region's Guild. Veterans can hide their map marks in Settings, Gameplay.
  • Older saves get the tutors too.
  • Your Send-Off mentor talks in a proper dialogue scene, with a "Tell me the basics first" option and a skip that asks before it takes effect. All five mentors talk about their own trades again.
  • Tutorial steps show as dots that tick off, and a First Steps card lists the basic controls, starting with the fact that time only moves when you act.
  • Lessons about hunger, thirst or manna outline that meter on the top bar, and the Quest Tracker shows the steps in your own keys or controller buttons.
  • Co-op guests see the mentor and tutors properly, and the lessons stay with the host.
  • You start with a full water pouch, unless a lesson is about to teach you to fill it.rubberychickensamv123

Dungeons in new worlds14

  • Every dungeon in a new world has a history. Its discovery line says who built it and why, what happened to it and who holds it now.
  • Rooms are furnished for what the place was built for, across fourteen kinds from mines and tombs to archives and aging caves, and larger set pieces like a sealed strongroom or a flooded pump room fit the floor they sit on.
  • Some floors hold a story of their own, like an expedition's abandoned camp with its journal, the aftermath of a battle or a rite cut short.
  • Doors can shut. People, the undead and constructs open them, beasts wait at them, and a shut door muffles the noise of a fight.
  • Some doors are locked, with the key somewhere else on the floor. A lockpick works too, but the door shuts again behind you.
  • Portcullises can bar a room, with the lever in another part of the floor, and a chest behind a locked door rolls Rare or better.
  • Hidden pressure plates fire darts or open a room of ambushers. Standing beside one gives you a chance each turn to spot it, better if you're sneaking.
  • In an idol vault, lifting the idol drops the gate behind you. There's a lever inside, which is fairer than most idols.
  • Every dungeon is checked when it's built, so a key or a lever can never block the way down, and you can always get out of an idol vault.
  • Every dungeon can reach 100%. Creatures behind a trap you haven't sprung don't count until it springs, and a boss's reinforcements never appear inside a secret room you haven't found.
  • Secret rooms open the first turn you stand next to their hidden door, and you feel a draught..knifeesamv123thesquirrel_74982
  • Teleporting and burrowing monsters can't hide in secret rooms you haven't found..knifee
  • New worlds have about 9,500 dungeons where they had about 7,000. A wild plant within eight tiles had been quietly ruling out one site in six.rubberychicken
  • Your first dungeon welcomes you in three short log lines where it used to be one long one.rubberychicken

Villages, towns and the people in them21

  • Villages, towns and cities in a new world feel lived in, with cluttered yards, benches and troughs on the green, stocked market counters and a readable sign on every shop.
  • Homes show who lives there, with a cradle by the parents' bed, a grandmother's chair by the fire or a loom for the family trade.
  • On a Crossing world, towns show their history, with a founder's statue, a burial ground with real names and years, and burnt houses where a place was sacked. Elders and priests tell the story, and the Chronicle has an Annals tab.
  • Paths wear across the grass to the well, and mud gathers round troughs and pens.
  • You can overhear townsfolk talking about rumours, their dead, their feuds, their children or something you did to them.
  • Ask "Where can I find..." and someone who knows will name the house and offer to walk you there.
  • The street reacts to your reputation. A victim's family glares at you, a guard warns a known thief and a trader greets a regular.
  • Towns keep living while you stay. Rumours spread, friendships grow and sour, couples marry, and news of deaths, births and raids travels to other towns.
  • Townsfolk keep every friendship, memory and mood when you save and continue.
  • Signposts are back in every settlement a new save builds, by each way in and by the market, pointing to the trades and to nearby towns. Every village has a general store, and every shop has a sign.samv123
  • Bakers sell bread, butchers sell meat and vintners sell cider. Bakers had been selling smithy goods.danekjovaxutc5
  • Night monsters keep well clear of new villages. On samv123's save, 21 spawns inside the village over 300 night turns dropped to none.samv123
  • Every place has a name of its own. The continent had 522 places sharing 207 names, including seven Lost Caverns, so places now take their names from their surroundings, like Foxguard Cave or Rock-Breaker's Pit. Existing saves are renamed to match, and rumour pins on the map use the new names.danekjovaxutc5
  • A town split by a river keeps its map mark, its Travel here target and its waystone at the market square, and the map doesn't draw a road through its streets.danekjovaxutc5
  • Fast travel never lands you in water, and if you're floating in deep water, you board the boat in your pack or swim for the nearest shore.danekjovaxutc5
  • Border skirmishes and frontier bandits gather in open country, away from town streets.rubberychicken
  • Escort travellers wait outside your house, and one whose contract has ended goes on alone.thesquirrel_74982
  • Townsfolk walk round trees and bushes..knifeesamv123
  • Foxes, wolves, bears and snow owls also come out at night where they live.danekjovaxutc5
  • Guards, innkeepers and scholars give directions in plain words, like how far to walk and how hard the dungeons are.nalathnidragon
  • Break out of a village lock-up and you can settle your fine with the innkeeper or the reeve. New villages keep no watch, so there used to be nobody to pay and your gear stayed locked away.

Roofs, night and sound4

  • Roofs are redrawn as pixel art in eight materials, matching across 2D, 2.5D and first person. Snow settles on the ridge first, and a chimney stands over every hearth.
  • At night, lit windows show who's still awake, lamplighters light the streets at dusk, and smoke only rises from chimneys with a fire going.
  • Settlements have their own sounds. The forge rings while the smith works, the inn gets louder at supper, and the market is busy by day and quiet at night.
  • Shrine bells toll at dawn, noon and dusk, crickets and owls come out after dark, and a rooster crows at dawn wherever hens are kept.

Fortress: the deep, the forge and the crown21

  • A new colony can dig 25 levels down through eight bands of the deep, from the Bedrock to the Undermaw at the floor of the world. A Shaft order digs a stair through several levels at once, and deeper bands hold new ores like palesilver and adamantine.
  • Working deep builds dread. Daylight, company, a lit hearth and a Shrine of the Green ease it, and a settler who reaches 100 is deep-touched for good.
  • Deep digging is loud. Lairs wake when they're seen or when the noise gets too high, and their creatures climb toward you. A body left below the caverns for three days rises as an echo, and the Deep panel (End) tracks it all.
  • The seal at the bottom of the Undermaw can be breached, after a confirmation. A breach brings waves and a spreading blight until you reseal it with iron and cut stone.
  • Any Adventure creature can raid a colony. Guards below ground answer nearby threats, and civilians run.
  • Wounds have stages. Minor ones heal with food and rest, serious ones give days of warning, and only a neglected severe wound kills.
  • Colony screens have a depth ruler, an alerts list sorted by urgency, map overlays and a selection card. The Build panel shows every building as its own art, with a suggested next step.
  • Smelters and forges turn ore into ingots, and the forge can make any Adventure weapon, shield or armour piece from them. Better smiths make better pieces, and a forge beside lava burns no fuel.
  • The Military panel (Shift+M) forms squads with banners, uniforms and duty hours. Order them to train, patrol, defend or retreat, or draft them and direct them by clicking.
  • Water and magma flow for real. They fall, spread, drown, burn and cool into obsidian, and floodgates, grates, levers and pumps let you steer them.
  • When you found a colony, you choose how its story is paced, from Gentle to Rising pressure, Unpredictable or Off. Raids, visitors, traders, weather and fevers arrive on that clock.
  • Terroir trances can take hold of a skilled smith, builder or cheesemaker, who then asks for materials. Give them what they ask and they make a named piece of gear or a legendary wheel. Refuse for five days and they break for good, though nobody dies of it.
  • Settlers earn epithets from their deeds, and the Chronicle tells your colony's history.
  • The World window has a War tab. March on any village, town or city with up to three squads and read battle reports that name who fell. Holdings pay tribute and can rebel, Guilds make war and peace, and there's a crown to win.
  • You can build a Gate in a wall line. People walk through, animals only pass while it's open, and raiders can't get past once it's braced.datsheeptho
  • Idle settlers stand calmly where they used to bob up and down like they were doing jumping jacks.datsheeptho
  • Goats and cows get watered when you raise the water order, and if they can't be, the warning names what's missing.datsheeptho
  • Each new system explains itself the first time it comes up, with one short hint.
  • Controllers work throughout, with Y for actions, the D-pad for depth and alerts, and the sticks for the cursor and camera.
  • A new colony's seven founders never share a first name, and cloud shadows stay on the surface.
  • Even a 25-level colony runs almost as fast as a three-level one.
